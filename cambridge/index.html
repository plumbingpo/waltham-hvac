How Cambridge Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

Small problems don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.

Common HVAC Problems in Cambridge

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. Hard-running summers push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and humidity and airborne grit slowly foul outdoor condenser coils, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Clogged filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

Our technicians also run into the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's mix of older homes brings undersized returns, leaky duct runs, and rooms the original system was never designed to condition.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are far cheaper to address early.
