When Your System Fails in Lowell

If your system quits, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls typically see a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

These are the problems that come up most when something breaks at home: warm air from the AC, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. Nearly all of these come down to parts already on our trucks: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and standard fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

A few minutes of caution before we arrive can prevent a much bigger repair. An iced-over AC should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us immediately.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Lowell

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that lasts and one that breaks down in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and also shows higher bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement in peak season.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
